Cases/TIMEPLACEME, Inc

Leading design for an AI-powered social gifting marketplace

TIMEPLACEME is a social gifting marketplace built around gifting experiences, with an AI-powered assistant that guides people through the gifting journey. I worked as Design Lead, shaping the product experience while mentoring a team of five designers.

01

Context

TIMEPLACEME is a social gifting marketplace: people gift experiences instead of things. It is a mobile B2B and B2C platform for European markets, with an AI-powered assistant that guides people through the gifting journey, from choosing an experience to sending it.

I joined in July 2025 as Design Lead on the project and stayed until February 2026, while the product was still taking shape.

02

The leadership challenge

The product was evolving fast, and five designers were working on it at the same time. The challenge was not only to design individual experiences, but to create enough clarity and consistency for multiple designers to work toward the same product direction.

  • Keep five designers moving in one direction while priorities were still changing.
  • Hold one level of quality and one visual language across everyone's work.
  • Make the AI assistant useful inside the gifting journey, not a separate gimmick.
03

My role

I owned
  • Design direction and priorities, set together with the founder
  • The AI assistant chat: conversation flows, UX logic and interaction patterns
  • Design quality across the team: reviews, feedback and hand-off
  • Coordinating and mentoring a team of five designers
I worked with
  • Tech, marketing and leadership, to align design with business goals
  • The team on iterative testing to grow engagement across European markets
04

How I led

Creating direction

I set design priorities with the founder and tied them to business goals, so every designer knew what mattered now and what could wait.

Design critique

I ran regular reviews and gave structured feedback, checking not only how a screen looks but whether it fits the product and is ready for hand-off.

Mentoring

I coordinated and mentored five designers. Reviews were also where I explained the reasoning behind decisions, so the team could make the next ones without me.

Collaboration

I worked with tech, marketing and leadership to keep design aligned with what the business needed and what could actually be built.

AI experience

I owned the AI assistant: its conversation flows, UX logic and interaction patterns along the gifting journey.

Key decisions

Decision 01

Priorities before screens

Context
Five designers, a product that changed quickly and many ideas competing for attention.
Decision
I agreed design priorities with the founder first and only then split the work between designers.
Why
Without a shared direction, five good designers produce five different products.
Decision 02

Reviews as the quality gate

Context
Work from several designers had to feel like one product and reach development in good shape.
Decision
I made regular design reviews the point where consistency, feedback and hand-off quality were checked.
Why
Catching inconsistencies in review is cheaper than fixing them after development.
Decision 03

An assistant that follows the journey

Context
Choosing an experience for someone else is harder than choosing a present in a shop.
Decision
I designed the AI assistant around the gifting journey itself, guiding people from choosing an experience to sending it.
Why
An assistant is useful when it helps with the next step, not when it lives in a separate corner of the app.
05

Outcome

The app is live on the App Store and Google Play. There are no public product metrics yet, so the clearest evidence of my contribution is how the founder, who managed my work directly, describes it:

“What impressed me most is her calm leadership and ability to navigate uncertainty, which is crucial in the early stages of any startup. I’m truly grateful for her contribution to TIMEPLACEME — both as a designer and as a person who brings positive energy, clarity, and structure to creative chaos.”

Ekaterina TsygankovaFounder, TIMEPLACEME · managed Olena directly · LinkedIn
06

What I learned

Design leadership is not about having the best answer for every screen. It is about creating enough clarity for a team to make good decisions consistently.

Mentoring makes the whole team faster. Every decision explained in a review is one the team can make on its own next time.

What I would do next

With the assistant live, I would look at where people drop out of the gifting journey and test whether the assistant helps them get to a sent gift faster than browsing on their own.
