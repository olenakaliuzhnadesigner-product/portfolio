Cases/Novakivsky Art Gallery

Ukraine's Van Gogh, given the website his paintings deserve

Oleksa Novakivskyi painted with the colour and nerve of the great expressionists, yet few people outside Lviv know his name. His great-granddaughter asked me to redesign the gallery's website. I designed a dark, quiet space where the paintings glow, with his life story, news and a way to support the project.

01

Context

Oleksa Novakivskyi (1872–1935) is often called the Ukrainian Van Gogh: thick, restless brushstrokes, burning colour, portraits that look straight through you. He founded the Lviv art school, yet his name is still far less known than it deserves to be.

The artist's great-granddaughter asked me to redesign the gallery's website: a living place to meet the artist, follow news and lectures, read about his life and support the project. This is a supporting case about visual storytelling and brand expression for a cultural product.

02

The challenge

Show the depth of the paintings in a way that is clear, accessible and alive, and bring news, lectures, a shop and donations into one calm structure that works on a phone.

03

Research and direction

I studied the old website, how leading museums present art online, and what visitors look for first. That shaped four decisions:

  1. The art comes first. Images get space, scale and a dark, neutral background.
  2. The story builds the connection. A biography split into periods, with photos and dates.
  3. Easy to find. Portraits, landscapes, still lifes and religious works, each one tap away.
  4. A clear way to help. “Support the project” visible on every page, without pushing.
04

Solution

A dark, gallery-like interface with one warm gold accent, so every canvas glows like it does on a museum wall. The headline sets the tone: «Живопис, який дихає життям», painting that breathes life.

05

What's next and what I learned

The collaboration continues: together with the artist's family and a developer, I'm now working on a new version of the website for a wider, international audience.

Design can step back. When the content is this strong, the best interface is the one you don't notice.
