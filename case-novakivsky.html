Cases/Novakivsky Art Gallery

Ukraine's Van Gogh, given the website his paintings deserve

Oleksa Novakivskyi painted with the colour and nerve of the great expressionists, yet few people outside Lviv know his name. His great-granddaughter asked me to redesign the gallery's website. I designed a dark, quiet space where the paintings glow, with his life story, news and a way to support the project.

01

Overview

Oleksa Novakivskyi (1872–1935) is often called the Ukrainian Van Gogh. Thick, restless brushstrokes, burning colour, portraits that look straight through you. He founded the Lviv art school and shaped a whole generation of painters, yet his name is still far less known than it deserves to be.

The artist's great-granddaughter came to me to redesign the gallery's old website. The goal was bigger than a portfolio of paintings: a living place to meet the artist, follow news, lectures and talks, read about his life, and support the project with a donation or by buying merchandise.

02

The challenge

Show the beauty and depth of the paintings in a way that is clear, accessible and alive, and let people feel why Novakivskyi stands next to the great expressionists.

  • Let the colour of the paintings lead, without the interface competing for attention.
  • Turn a long, dense biography into a story people actually read.
  • Bring news, lectures, a shop and donations into one calm structure.
  • Work equally well on a phone, where most visitors arrive from social media.
03

Research

Before drawing anything, I studied the old website and how people moved through it, looked at how leading museums and galleries present art online, and listened to what visitors expect: where they get lost, what they look for first, and what makes them stay.

  1. The art comes first. People come to see paintings, so images need space, scale and a dark, neutral background.
  2. The story builds the connection. A biography split into periods, with photos and dates, is read far more than one long text.
  3. Easy to find, easy to filter. Portraits, landscapes, still lifes and religious works, each one tap away.
  4. A clear way to help. "Support the project" stays visible on every page, without pushing.
04

Solution

A dark, gallery-like interface with one warm gold accent, so every canvas glows like it does on a museum wall. The headline sets the tone: «Живопис, який дихає життям», painting that breathes life.

05

What's next

The collaboration continues. Together with the artist's family and a developer, I'm now building a new version of the website, adapted to how people browse today and ready to bring Novakivskyi to a wider, international audience.

06

What I learned

Design can step back. When the content is this strong, the best interface is the one you don't notice.

Stories need structure. A life split into periods, photos and dates turns a museum text into something people want to read.

Working with a family's legacy means listening carefully: every decision is also about memory and respect.
