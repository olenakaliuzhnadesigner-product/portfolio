Cases/VinAssessment

Making complex automotive data easier to understand and act on

VinAssessment brings vehicle history and data to US car dealers and buyers. I worked across dashboards, listings and a browser extension to make dense information easier to scan, understand and use.

01

Context

Buying a used car is a bet on its past. Was it in an accident? Sold at auction? How many owners? VinAssessment collects that history by VIN and turns it into a report: auction photos, title records, previous owners, listing and price history.

Two audiences use it. US car dealers check dozens of cars a day and track their own sales. Private buyers want to know if one car is safe to buy. Around the dealer platform grew two consumer products, connected by one browser extension.

B2B · dealersVinAssessment ↗Vehicle history reports, window stickers and a dealer dashboard. B2C · buyersVehiSales ↗A marketplace to browse and compare used cars across the US. B2C · buyersVehiLeaks ↗Vehicle history reports for buyers, with a Chrome extension.
02

The challenge

The product held a huge amount of vehicle information: millions of records, auction photos, title brands, odometer readings. But people needed to see the most important signals quickly and decide without getting lost in data: is this car a good deal, or a risk?

  • Turn dense data into reports people can scan, not study.
  • Give dealers one place for reports, window stickers and their own sales statistics.
  • Keep three products with different audiences visibly related, so each one builds trust in the others.

04

What I found

I worked through the Double Diamond: market and user research, personas and product hypotheses, then prototyping and testing. I compared 10 VIN services across 11 features. Four things stood out:

  1. Generic reports dominate. Legacy players rely on brand recognition, not product experience.
  2. Dealer workflows are underserved. Dealers are high-value users, yet lack tools that fit their daily browsing.
  3. Browser extensions are rare. Almost no one offers one.
  4. Brand over experience. Competitors invest little in modern UX, which left room to win on clarity.
Key insight

Dealers already spend their day on listing sites like Craigslist, Autotrader, Cars.com and CarMax. The product should come to them there, instead of asking them to copy a VIN into another tab for every car.

05

Key design decisions

Decision 01

Bring the history into the listing

Problem
Checking a car meant copying its VIN into another tab, for every car, all day.
Decision
Dealers asked for it, and the research backed it up. I designed a Chrome extension that reads the VIN on the listing and shows the report next to it.
Why
Extensions were rare among competitors and dealers' workflows were underserved, so this was the biggest opportunity.
Result
Three steps instead of a context switch: open a listing, click the extension, see the history.
Decision 02

Show auction photos next to listing photos

Problem
Hidden damage is the main risk, and it is easy to miss in a long report.
Decision
I placed auction photos side by side with the current listing photos.
Why
A visual comparison answers “is this a risk?” faster than any table.
Decision 03

One dashboard, two audiences

Problem
Dealers check whole lots and track sales; private buyers check one car.
Decision
I designed a single dashboard that scales from one VIN to many: single search, multi-VIN search with a history table, and dealer statistics.
Why
One structure is easier to learn, build and maintain than two separate products.
Decision 04

One system across three products

Problem
Three products for different audiences could easily drift apart.
Decision
I built one component library with responsive grids for 1440, 768 and 390 px and reused it across all three.
Why
Shared patterns let the products ship faster and still feel related, so trust in one carries over to the others.

07

Systems thinking

  • Information hierarchy. Reports built to be scanned, not studied.
  • Structure. A site map of eight main sections for the dealer platform.
  • Components. One library reused across all three products.
  • Responsive behaviour. One grid system for 1440, 768 and 390 px.
  • Even edge cases. Friendly 404 and 505 pages that lead people back.

09

What I learned

When a product holds a lot of information, the challenge is rarely to display more of it. It is to help people understand what matters, when it matters and what they can do next.

Meet people where they already are. The extension worked because it didn't ask dealers to change how they browse.

What I would do next

I would measure how often dealers open a report from the extension versus the website, and use that to decide which surface gets the next improvements.
