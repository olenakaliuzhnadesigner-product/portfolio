Cases/VinAssessment

One designer, three automotive products and a Chrome extension

VinAssessment turns raw vehicle history into reports that car dealers can read in seconds. Around it grew two consumer products: VehiSales, a marketplace for used cars, and VehiLeaks, vehicle history reports for buyers. I designed all three, plus the Chrome extension that brings a report onto any car listing.

01

Overview

Buying a used car is a bet on its past. Was it in an accident? Sold at auction? How many owners? VinAssessment collects that history by VIN and turns it into a report a dealer can read in seconds: auction photos, title records, previous owners, listing and price history.

I joined as the designer and ended up shaping a small ecosystem. One platform for dealers, two products for car buyers, and a Chrome extension that connects them all.

02

The challenge

Vehicle history data is huge and messy: millions of records, auction photos, title brands, odometer readings. Dealers don't have time to dig through it. They need an answer at a glance: is this car a good deal, or a risk?

  • Turn complex data into reports people can scan, not study.
  • Give dealers one place for reports, window stickers and their own sales statistics.
  • Keep three products with different audiences visually related, so each one builds trust in the others.

VehiLeaks

VehiLeaks helps dealers and private buyers check a car's data, history and specs in seconds, by VIN or license plate. I owned the product end to end: UX, the user dashboard, the admin panel and the data-heavy UI behind every report.

The main problem we set out to solve: people struggle to find a reliable, fast and clear way to verify VIN data and avoid hidden damage.

Research

I worked through the Double Diamond: market analysis and user research, personas and product hypotheses, then prototyping, testing, final UI and a design system. A competitor analysis of 10 VIN services across 11 features showed four things:

  1. Generic reports dominate. Legacy players rely on brand recognition, not product experience.
  2. Dealer workflows are underserved. Dealers are high-value users, yet lack tools that fit their daily browsing.
  3. Browser extensions are rare. Almost no one offers one, so it became our biggest opportunity.
  4. Brand over experience. Competitors invest little in modern UX, which left room to win on clarity.

Solution

A single dashboard for both audiences. Search one VIN or a whole group, open a report with auction and listing photos side by side, and track sales for a dealership.

08

What I learned

Data needs a story. The same records feel overwhelming in a table and obvious in a well-ordered report.

Meet people where they already are. The extension worked because it didn't ask dealers to change how they browse.

Systems scale products. One set of components let three products ship faster and still feel related.
