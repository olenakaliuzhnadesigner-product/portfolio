02 · The challenge

Two kinds of trust,
in one app

Most people don't read security claims and don't know what P2P encryption or a seed phrase is. The challenge was not to design individual screens, but to make a complex, high-stakes product feel obvious and safe.

Features grew faster than our picture of what people needed. The team needed evidence: which parts of the product build trust, and which quietly break it.

💬
Trust with conversations

“Can anyone read this?”

Encryption, self-destructing messages and P2P calls only matter if people feel them, not just read about them.

💸
Trust with money

“Did it arrive? Did I lose it?”

Self-custody, networks, fees and stablecoins: every label and confirmation either builds trust or breaks it.

Study 01 · Usability testing before launch · 2025

11 people, 7 tasks, one uncomfortable truth

We were three product designers. Together we built personas, wrote the interview script and tasks, ran the sessions and synthesised the results. I took part at every stage: I interviewed participants, took notes, synthesised findings task by task and co-presented the recommendations to the team.

Each session: a short interview about how people communicate at work and in life, then tasks from finding the app in the App Store to sending an invite link.

Key insight

People don't trust text. Trust has to be built before someone opens the app, then confirmed by a product that feels obvious.

The findings became a round of changes to onboarding, contacts, calls, chats and security messaging, and shaped my work on the website and the store pages.
From findings to direction

What we recommended

Every finding became a concrete change, presented to the team task by task.

Who are we?Who is it for?Why choose us?

The three questions we left the team with. The website restructure is my answer to the last one.

  1. Explain security simply, before the appShort, plain-language proof in the store and on the site instead of terms like P2P.
  2. A home screen with clear ways to add a contactRemove the confusing link widget and the dialer.
  3. One Calls tabMerge calls and meetings, with group calls inside.
  4. A shorter security messageOne line, a “learn more” link, hidden after a few seconds.
  5. Disappearing messages as an optionChats stay by default; the timer is a choice.
  6. Avatars and calmer callsProfile pictures instead of a big logo, clearer button states.
Study 02 · Beta research for the wallet · 2026

31 beta sessions, turned into new flows

Our team ran 31 beta sessions with real users. We synthesised the results together, and as the sole product designer I turn them into redesigned flows.

4 of 6 testers mistook the token price for the money they hold.

06 · Key design decisions

Three decisions
that shaped the product

Decision 01

Keep payments inside the conversation

Problem
Paying someone in crypto meant copying wallet addresses, switching apps and buying a separate coin for the network fee: slow and easy to get wrong.
Decision
We made sending money an action of the chat itself: next to the message field, with the transfer showing up as a message and the fee paid in the same stablecoin.
Why
That is how people actually used the wallet: to pay a person they were already talking to.
Result
Sending money became one flow of five steps without leaving the conversation, and the centre of the product.
Decision 02

Build trust before the app opens

Problem
Testing showed that people skip store descriptions and don't trust security claims written as text.
Decision
I restructured extrasafe.chat around one message, “the first messenger built on money rails”, with a clear hierarchy and one next step. The store screenshots got the same approach: show how the product protects you, not a list of features.
Why
People arrive with an opinion already formed. The website and the store page are where trust starts.
Result
One clear story from the first screen to the download button, used across the site, the stores and marketing.
Decision 03

One system for the whole ecosystem

Problem
The app, the main site, three sister products and a steady stream of marketing visuals risked feeling like separate brands.
Decision
I built a unified visual identity and a component library shared by marketing and development.
Why
A product that changes every few months needs a system, not one-off pages.
Result
Marketing builds new assets from ready components instead of drawing from scratch. The wallet, new networks and new markets were added without redrawing everything.

12 · What I learned

What I take with me,
and what I'd change

01

In fintech, clarity is a security feature

A vague label costs more than a missing feature: when money and private messages live in one app, every label, confirmation and empty state either builds trust or breaks it.

02

Research works as a habit, not a project

Test, change, test again. The move from “secure messenger” to “money in chat” came from watching how people actually used the product.

03

A design system is a business tool

It decides how fast a company can say yes to a new feature or a new market.

04

What I'd do differently: recruit for one audience

Only 2 of our 11 participants matched the target audience. Next time I'd define one segment first and recruit to it, so every finding speaks for the people we build for.
