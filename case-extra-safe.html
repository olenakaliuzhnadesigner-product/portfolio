01

Context

Extra Safe is a privacy-first messenger with a built-in stablecoin wallet. People chat, call and share files with end-to-end encryption, and when they need to pay someone, they send USDT and other stablecoins right in the conversation. No copying wallet addresses, no switching apps, no buying a separate coin to pay the network fee.

I joined in November 2024, when Extra Safe was an idea and a rough draft. I built the brand from scratch and stayed with the product as it grew from an encrypted chat with calls into a messenger where money moves like a message. Today I'm its sole product designer.

02

The challenge

Extra Safe asks people for two kinds of trust at once: trust with their conversations and trust with their money. Most people don't read security claims and don't know what P2P encryption or a seed phrase is. The challenge was not to design individual screens, but to make a complex, high-stakes product feel obvious and safe.

And the ground kept moving. Every few months a new layer appeared: calls, file sharing, a wallet, private balances, new networks.

04

Research loop

01Test with people02Synthesise03Change the product04Test again

We don't run research once. We test, change the product, then test again with people who already use the app, and repeat. Two studies show how this works.

Usability testing before launch · 2025

We were three product designers. Together we built personas, wrote the interview script and tasks, ran the sessions and synthesised the results. I took part at every stage: I interviewed participants, took notes, synthesised findings task by task and co-presented the recommendations to the team.

We shortlisted 24 people against our personas and ran 11 sessions: a short interview, then 7 tasks across the core flows, from finding the app in the App Store to sending an invite link.

8 of 11didn't read the App Store description, and 5 said it didn't help them feel safe
8 of 11pressed the Link button expecting to add a contact
8 of 11saw the encryption banner and felt no safer
10 of 11couldn't tell Calls from Meetings
6 of 11disliked messages disappearing by default
4 of 11said they would stop using the app at that point
Key insight

People don't trust text. Trust has to be built before someone opens the app, then confirmed by a product that feels obvious. The findings became a round of changes to onboarding, contacts, calls, chats and security messaging, and shaped my work on the website and the store pages.

Beta research for the wallet · 2026

Our team ran 31 beta sessions with real users. We synthesised the results together, and as the sole product designer I turn them into redesigned flows.

  • Price looked like balance4 of 6 testers mistook the token price for the money they hold.
  • Private tokens were unclearOnly 1 in 10 people understood them without help.
  • No sign the money arrivedPeople left the send screen without knowing whether the transfer landed.
  • Sending from chat went unnoticedMany didn't know they could send money right from a conversation.

What I'm redesigning from it: simpler wallet onboarding; a send flow that shows who you're paying and the full fee before you confirm; a notice when money arrives; a wallet screen that shows the balance, not the price; the blog, help centre and community reachable from inside the app.

05

Key design decisions

Decision 01

Keep payments inside the conversation

Problem
Paying someone in crypto meant copying wallet addresses, switching apps and buying a separate coin for the network fee: slow and easy to get wrong.
Decision
We made sending money an action of the chat itself: next to the message field, with the transfer showing up as a message and the fee paid in the same stablecoin.
Why
That is how people actually used the wallet: to pay a person they were already talking to.
Result
Sending money became one flow of five steps without leaving the conversation, and the centre of the product.
Decision 02

Build trust before the app opens

Problem
Testing showed that people skip store descriptions and don't trust security claims written as text.
Decision
I restructured extrasafe.chat around one message, “the first messenger built on money rails”, with a clear hierarchy and one next step. The store screenshots got the same approach: show how the product protects you, not a list of features.
Why
People arrive with an opinion already formed. The website and the store page are where trust starts.
Result
One clear story from the first screen to the download button, used across the site, the stores and marketing.
Decision 03

One system for the whole ecosystem

Problem
The app, the main site, three sister products and a steady stream of marketing visuals risked feeling like separate brands.
Decision
I built a unified visual identity and a component library shared by marketing and development.
Why
A product that changes every few months needs a system, not one-off pages.
Result
Marketing builds new assets from ready components instead of drawing from scratch. The wallet, new networks and new markets were added without redrawing everything.

09

What I learned

In fintech, clarity is a security feature. A vague label costs more than a missing feature: when money and private messages live in one app, every label, confirmation and empty state either builds trust or breaks it.

Research works as a habit, not a project. Test, change, test again. The move from “secure messenger” to “money in chat” came from watching how people actually used the product.

A design system is a business tool. It decides how fast a company can say yes to a new feature or a new market.
