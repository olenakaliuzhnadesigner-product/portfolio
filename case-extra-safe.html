Cases/Extra Safe

Designing clarity across a complex fintech and communication ecosystem

Extra Safe combines private communication, identity and financial functionality in one product ecosystem. My role was to help make that complexity understandable, consistent and usable across multiple surfaces.

01

Context

Extra Safe is a privacy-focused messenger with a built-in stablecoin wallet. People chat, call and share files with end-to-end encryption, and when they need to pay someone, they send USDT or ETH right in the conversation. No copying wallet addresses, no switching apps.

I joined in November 2024, almost a year before the public launch, and stayed with the product as it grew from a private chat with calls into a messenger built on money rails.

02

The challenge

The product combined several complex concepts — private communication, wallet functionality, identity and financial actions — across different platforms. The challenge was not to design individual screens, but to create one coherent experience people could understand and trust.

And the ground kept moving. Every few months a new layer appeared: calls, file sharing, a wallet, private balances, cross-chain swaps.

04

What we found

01Interview users02Synthesise03Change the product04Ship & measure

We regularly interviewed users, brought the findings together and turned them into concrete changes in the app and on the website. Each round fed the next release.

The biggest shift came from this loop. The first versions were a classic secure messenger. As we learned how people actually used it, the product moved toward payments.

Key insight

The wallet works on its own, but its real point is sending money to a person the same way you send a message. And when money and private messages live in one app, every label, confirmation and empty state either builds trust or breaks it.

05

Key design decisions

Decision 01

Keep payments inside the conversation

Problem
Paying someone in crypto meant copying wallet addresses and switching apps, which is slow and easy to get wrong.
Decision
Together with the product designer, we made sending money an action of the chat itself: next to the message field, with the transfer showing up as a message.
Why
That is how people actually used the wallet: to pay a person they were already talking to.
Result
Sending money became one flow of five steps without leaving the conversation, and the centre of the product.
Decision 02

Restructure the website around one message

Problem
The product had many layers — chat, calls, wallet, a blog, a Knowledge Hub — and the website had to explain it to people who had never heard of it.
Decision
I restructured extrasafe.chat around a clear content hierarchy: one message, “the first messenger built on money rails”, and a clear path to download.
Why
Visitors decide fast. Navigation and content clarity mattered more than showing every feature.
Result
~20–30% increase in conversion to key actions.
Decision 03

One system for the whole ecosystem

Problem
The app, the main site, three sister products and a steady stream of marketing visuals risked feeling like separate brands, and producing them slowed the team down.
Decision
I introduced a unified visual identity and a component library shared by marketing and development.
Why
A product that changes every few months needs a system, not one-off pages.
Result
~36% less time to produce marketing assets. The wallet, new networks and new markets were added without redrawing everything.

09

What I learned

Complex products rarely become simple by removing information alone. The more important work is deciding what people need to understand now, what can wait, and how the system stays consistent as the product grows.

Talking to users beats guessing. The move from “secure messenger” to “money in chat” came from listening to how people actually used the product.

What I would do next

With more product data, I would keep validating the highest-friction flows and prioritise improvements by user behaviour rather than visual polish alone.
